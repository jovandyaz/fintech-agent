## Why the injection scan is only a signal

"The Attacker Moves Second" (2025) ran adaptive attacks against 12 published defenses against prompt injection and jailbreaks. It reports attack success above 90% for most of them, and 100% for human red-teamers, against defenses that had reported near-zero rates. Source: [Willison's summary, 2025-11-02](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Nov/2/new-prompt-injection-papers/); the paper itself was not opened.

A heuristic scan is weaker than those defenses, so it is not a guarantee here. At intake it sets `injection_signal`: the operator must acknowledge the flag, it counts as a fraud signal for `escalate_fraud`, and it feeds an alert. It never blocks a case. At ingestion a hit quarantines the chunk, which fails safe because the corpus is ours (G8). What holds when the model fully obeys an injection is the rest of the [defense model](../specs/02-security.md#defense-model).

## MCP authorization deviation (02 G4)

MCP authorization is optional in the [2026-07-28 revision](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog). When used, it is OAuth 2.1 with protected resource metadata (RFC 9728) and audience binding (RFC 8707). This build runs no authorization server. The harness mints a case token instead and keeps the parts that matter here:

- audience bound to the server's canonical URL (`MCP_AUDIENCE`), checked with issuer and algorithm;
- no passthrough: the MCP server calls core-mock with its own read-only key ([MCP Security Best Practices](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/draft/basic/security_best_practices));
- a short life: `RUN_TIMEOUT_MS` + 60 s, never more than 10 minutes;
- a rate limit: the 13th call on one token returns `RATE_LIMITED`, as the [tools spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/server/tools) requires servers to rate limit.

Why: client and server sit in one trust domain. Production makes the MCP server an OAuth resource server; DESIGN.md says so.

## Personal data (LFPDPPP)

Official text: [LFPDPPP](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LFPDPPP.pdf), new law published DOF 2025-03-20, last reform DOF 2025-11-14, read 2026-10-10.

### The LLM provider as a processor

- **Position.** Art. 2 fr. XII defines the _persona encargada_: whoever processes personal data on behalf of the _responsable_. Fr. XX leaves a communication to the encargada out of a _transferencia_. The provider stands there: an _encargado_ receiving a _remisión_ (02 G6).
- **What the contract must say before production.** The provider processes only on albo's behalf (art. 2 fr. XII) and for the purposes in albo's aviso (art. 11). It does not train on albo's data. It keeps nothing: zero data retention. Its confidentiality outlives the contract (art. 20). Its security measures are at least those art. 18 requires of albo. These are requirements for the contract, not a statement of any provider's current terms.
- **What the design already limits.** The provider receives masked text only: the redactor reads `text_masked`, the agent reads `text_redacted` and masked tool outputs. Tool outputs carry a first name only, and no full CLABE, card, phone, RFC, CURP or auth factor reaches the provider (G6, [01 §Tools](../specs/01-architecture.md#tools)). Raw text is never stored.
- **Not confirmed:** whether a new LFPDPPP regulation covers cloud processors (references.md).

### Arts. 14 and 26 fr. II

- **Art. 14.** The responsable must tell the titular, through the _aviso de privacidad_, that the processing exists and what its main characteristics are, so the titular can decide with that information. For this build: whether albo's current aviso describes AI-assisted drafting and the provider as encargado is an open question.
- **Art. 26 fr. II.** The titular may oppose, or demand an end to, automated processing that produces unwanted legal effects or significantly affects their interests, rights or freedoms and is meant to evaluate personal aspects without human intervention (economic situation, reliability, behavior among them). For this build: no outcome reaches a customer without a person. An authenticated operator approves or rejects every action and signs off every reply (G3); the agent only proposes.

## Card data (PCI DSS)

PCI scope stays out of the provider (02 G6):

- **Display.** Last 4 only, stricter than PCI DSS 3.4.1 (BIN + last 4 at most).
- **Storage.** Masking at intake and at the MCP boundary keeps the provider out of PAN storage (3.5.1). Masking is fail-closed: a Luhn-invalid or altered number is masked too.
- **AI.** The [PCI SSC AI principles](https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/ai-principles-securing-the-use-of-ai-in-payment-environments) ask to limit what the model receives, prefer truncated values and filter its output: G6 and `PII_IN_REPLY` do that.
- **No image path.** The webhook and the console accept text only.

Sources: [PCI SSC Summary of Changes v3.2.1 → v4.0](https://listings.pcisecuritystandards.org/documents/PCI-DSS-v3-2-1-to-v4-0-Summary-of-Changes-r1.pdf) and [SAQ D v4.0](https://listings.pcisecuritystandards.org/documents/PCI-DSS-v4-0-SAQ-D-Merchant.pdf); the v4.0.1 text is behind the license wall and was not read.

## Benchmarks (not binding in Mexico)

**EU AI Act.** [Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/) point 5(b) lists as high-risk AI that evaluates creditworthiness or sets a credit score, except fraud detection. This agent scores no credit, so it is not high-risk. [Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)(1), applicable from 2026-08-02, asks providers of AI systems that interact directly with people to tell them so. Here the customer never interacts with the agent: an operator reads, edits and signs off the reply.

**Reg E ([12 CFR 1005.11](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/11/)).** US error resolution: the consumer gives oral or written notice within 60 days of the statement; the institution investigates within 10 business days, or up to 45 days if it credits provisionally within 10 business days. The Mexican rules the design follows are 90 natural days to file (LTOSF art. 23 fr. I), a dictamen within 45 days (fr. II) and, for an unrecognized card charge, a credit by the 2nd business day unless two factors are proven (Circ. 12/2018 18.a). Both clocks start at the customer's notice, which is why the acuse goes out at intake without waiting for the agent.

## Disclosure of AI-drafted replies

## Open questions for Legal
